From the November 4, 2026 LITT Briefing

The Layoff Notice Is Becoming an AI Disclosure

Picture the person at your organization who signs the next WARN notice. Near the top of the form is a new question: were these layoffs related to the use of artificial intelligence? Whoever signs is making a statement of causation to the government. In California, the state will publish a summary of it.

That's already the law in Connecticut. Since October 1, employers filing a federal WARN notice must tell the state Labor Department whether the layoffs relate to the employer's use of AI or another technological change. The citation is Section 26 of Public Act 26-15 (Connecticut Department of Labor).

California goes further. SB 951, signed by Gov. Gavin Newsom on September 30, takes effect January 1, 2027. For layoffs caused "in whole or in substantial part" by AI, the Cal-WARN notice must open with the line "This notice is for a technology displacement." It must also identify the positions affected, the job functions being automated, and the type of technology used (CDF Labor Law; Ogletree).

New York tried a version of this first, and the result is worth sitting with. The state added a "technological innovation or automation" checkbox to its WARN filings in March 2025. In the year that followed, more than 160 companies filed notices. None attributed the layoffs to technology or automation (Bloomberg Law). Andi Mazingo of Lumen Law Center offered one reading: "Companies are using narratives about AI to frame layoffs in ways that are most likely to be well received by shareholders and potential shareholders."

Maybe none of those layoffs were caused by AI. Maybe some were, and nobody wanted that sentence in a filing. A checkbox can't tell the difference, and neither can the state. Connecticut and California have now written the question into the statute.

For most employers, this isn't about mass layoffs next quarter. It's about the scheduling, billing, intake, and documentation work AI is absorbing now. If roles change gradually and nobody writes down why, the organization will have to reconstruct a causation story under deadline later, and staff will already have drawn their own conclusions.

This week's focus is narrow. If you operate in Connecticut or California, confirm who owns WARN filings. Then start a simple log of every role where AI is taking on tasks, what changed, and whether headcount moved. That log is the record you'll want when a regulator, an employee, or a board member asks.
