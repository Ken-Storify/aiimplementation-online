From the November 18, 2026 LITT Briefing

The Claim Came First

The investigation opened on a Sunday. On Monday, a shareholder filed a securities class action against Nvidia (Business Wire). On Wednesday, a derivative suit named CEO Jensen Huang and every other director (Bloomberg Law). On Thursday, Huang resigned (CBS News).

The claims did not wait for the facts. They did not wait for the CEO to leave.

The derivative complaint alleges the directors breached their duty of oversight by "consciously ignoring red flags" about the CEO, a customer under export-control investigation, and a foreign intelligence threat (Insurance Journal). Marsh's Will Fahey told Business Insurance it is a contender for the largest derivative claim ever (Business Insurance).

Read it as an oversight story, not a scandal story. And oversight is where AI risk lands on your board. When an AI tool in resident care fails, the question will be the one Jonathan Ziss of Goldberg Segalla asked about Nvidia: "what did they know and when did they know it" (Business Insurance).

The coverage questions are not simple. Sarah Abrams of RT ProExec's OakBridge team told The Insurer she expected Huang to have separate Side A cover, the part of a D&O program that protects directors and officers personally (The Insurer). The Insurer has since reported that Side A is all Nvidia bought: an estimated $800 million to $950 million for its people, and nothing for the company itself (The Insurer). Nvidia has not confirmed it.

Most operators have never mapped which part of their own program answers which claim. And D&O forms are already being written with AI exclusions. The W. R. Berkley "absolute" AI exclusion I covered on August 25 was drafted for D&O, E&O, and fiduciary liability.

The defense is a record. Ann Lipton, a University of Colorado law professor, told Business Insurance that plaintiffs must show bad faith, and that the board's decision to act and bring in an investigator could help it. I call that record the Red Flag Ledger: what you saw about your AI tools, who heard it, and what you decided. Build it with counsel, because a written record can be discoverable.

This month, start the 30-Day AI Coverage and Contract Audit with three questions for your broker. Has your carrier filed an AI exclusion endorsement in your states? Does your policy respond to a claim from an AI failure? Have you reviewed vendor contracts for indemnification language? Then add a fourth for D&O: how much Side A limit do your directors personally have?
