From the November 11, 2026 LITT Briefing

The Policy Now Asks Who Approved It

Picture the next renewal meeting. The broker opens the supplemental application, and there's a new page. Do you keep an inventory of your AI tools? Who is responsible for each one? The CFO looks across the table at operations. Nobody has that list.

Some carriers are already asking. Insurance Business reports that supplemental applications now probe AI governance, including whether a company keeps an inventory of its AI tools and who is responsible for each one. In the same article, Lockton's Ashley Bennett Jones said agent-to-agent transactions leave less room for a human reviewer to "exist in a meaningful way," and that some carriers may want to underwrite and price that exposure differently (Insurance Business).

Eight days later, a carrier turned that idea into a coverage trigger. Cowbell announced a standalone AI liability policy for small and mid-sized businesses, launching in January 2027 through wholesale brokers on a non-admitted basis. It responds to what Cowbell calls an AI incident, including when an AI tool exceeds its authorized scope or "acts without required approval" (Insurance Business). Terms are pre-launch, and final wording will govern.

Read that trigger again. It assumes an AI agent can act without approval. Can yours? And if it can, who decided that?

Regulators are working on the same question. On November 16, the NAIC's Big Data and Artificial Intelligence Working Group is scheduled to consider adopting version 7.0 of its AI Risk Evaluation Supplement in Dallas. That vote hasn't happened yet (NAIC). According to one tracker, the draft adds a "Human in the Loop" section that asks whether a reviewer can actually reverse an AI-assisted decision, and whether overrides show up in the record (InsureAI Wire). That section is about how regulators examine insurers. It's still a good test for any policyholder.

Jones also warned against starting with insurance. Assess what your agents can do. Reduce the exposure. Push what you can into vendor contracts, and check that the vendor can pay for what it promises. Insurance covers what's left after that.

This month, add one question to your 30-Day AI Coverage and Contract Audit: "If one of our AI agents acts without required approval and a loss results, which policy responds, and where is that answer in writing?" Get the answer in writing. A verbal assurance won't help you when the claim comes in.
