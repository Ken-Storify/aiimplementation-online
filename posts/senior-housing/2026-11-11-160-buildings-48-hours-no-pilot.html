From the November 11, 2026 LITT Briefing

160 Buildings. 48 Hours. No Pilot.

Picture an admissions coordinator in a rural Texas nursing home on launch morning. Yesterday she worked a fax machine and a handful of referral portals she knew by heart. Today all 160 of her company's buildings switched to the same AI platform at once. There's no sister facility to call, because every one of them is learning it too.

That's what Creative Solutions in Healthcare says it did. The Fort Worth operator launched exacare ai's referral and admissions platform across 160 skilled nursing facilities. More than 3,000 users came online within 48 hours, eight weeks after the agreement was signed, with no pilot facility first (Creative Solutions release via Business Wire).

It reports referral response times down more than 90% and acceptance rates up from 52% to 60%. Weigh the source. Those figures come from a company press release about an award the vendor itself gives, and the release lists the operator at both 160 facilities and more than 155 communities. Treat the numbers as self-reported.

Here's what is worth learning. Before the switch, the company ran referrals through about 20 separate systems. It trained more than 3,000 users ahead of go-live. That training likely did the work a pilot usually does.

It's not a story about speed. It's a story about what replaces the pilot. My book tells operators to start small: one unit, one use case, ninety days. But those ninety days were never a technology test. They are a test of whether your organization can govern AI in production. A pilot proves the tool works, catches what breaks, and gives staff a safe way to say so. Skip it, and you have to name what does each of those jobs instead.

There's also a question hidden in the good number. Going from 52% to 60% acceptance means saying yes to eight more of every hundred referrals. That's a growth decision. Someone has to make sure faster yeses aren't turning into yeses the building can't staff.

Not everyone is buying. CarDon & Associates' chief technology officer told Skilled Nursing News his company is investing in building AI tools in-house, with a referral intake platform in the works. The reporter's summary of his advice: take a measured approach, and review vendor agreements carefully (Skilled Nursing News). Buy it and go fast, or build it and go measured. Both are leadership decisions. Neither should happen by default.

This month, before any AI go-live, write down three things: what your pilot was supposed to prove, the clinical-fit measure you'll track next to acceptance rate, and the name of the executive who can call a rollback.
